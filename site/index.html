Three systems, one pipeline

From pixels to positions.

01

Tape Reader

Computer vision that read four years of a livestream so I didn't have to.

  • Built withPython · yt-dlp · ffmpeg · RapidOCR / ONNX Runtime · multiprocessing

Every trading morning a YouTube stream scrolls an alert feed of the day's premarket movers. Nobody publishes that list. So I built a pipeline that watches the whole archive for me.

For each day it finds the feed in the video, reads the stream's on-screen clock to work out the premarket window, downloads just those two and a half hours in parallel chunks, and OCRs a thin band of the feed across a 12-process pool. Every read is checked against the listed-symbol universe and kept only on evidence.

  • No timezone maths anywhere. Each day's window comes from the clock printed on the stream itself, which handles a start time that drifts from 06:05 to 07:37 ET and a panel that moved 35 pixels between eras.
  • It knows where its data ends. An 8-second probe gates each 2.5-hour download. Three consecutive misses mark the archive's layout boundary, which it found on its own: April 2022.
  • Precision over recall. Scroll-clipped partials (NOW from NOWL) die to prefix dominance, one-off reads need 0.65 confidence (calibrated on audited crops), and delisted symbols need stronger evidence still.
  • Throughput. YouTube throttles each connection to about 1.7× realtime, so each day downloads in parallel chunks. Static frames are dropped before OCR. One day takes about 75 seconds.

02

Hindsight

A tick-accurate backtester, engineered to have none.

  • Built withPython 3.13 · pandas · NumPy · IBKR API · SQLite · pytest · GitHub Actions

Most backtests lie in small ways. They fill at prices that never traded, or let a 08:15 decision peek at 08:20's volume. Hindsight is built so it can't.

It replays each premarket on IBKR 10-second bars and resolves every candle that crosses a trigger, stop or target from the real trades inside it, then adds order latency. Every entry gate reads only data through the prior completed minute. The result is a backtest you can actually port to live trading, and a live bot that later did.

  • Tick-resolved fills. The fill is the first real print after the crossing, plus 100 ms. Targets fill at the crossing print, because a resting limit doesn't wait. That distinction once turned real wins into phantom break-evens. The microscope below re-derives this trade's fills from the raw ticks, in your browser, and checks them against the backtest.
  • Causal by construction. Volume, MACD, EMA and VWAP gates all read the prior completed minute. A volume surge that hasn't happened yet can't qualify a trade.
  • A cache that heals itself. 14.5 GB of bars and ticks. An empty tick response is never cached as truth: a gap memo retries across runs, then retires windows IBKR will never serve.
  • Research-grade output. Interactive HTML reports, per-trade charts, SQLite, Monte Carlo, t-statistics, confidence intervals, and concentration checks: what if the best three trades never happened?

Profitable isn't the same as real.

The backtester ran two strategies, and both made money in backtest. Only one held up: the wedge's 95% confidence interval straddles zero (t = 0.51) and its live paper results came in below expectation, so it was switched off despite a profitable backtest. The dip's interval clears zero, and its mean stays positive with its five best trades deleted.

Backtest research figures in R (multiples of risk per trade), not live returns.

03

Parity

A live trading bot that must make the same decisions as its backtest, or it refuses to run.

  • Built withPython 3.13 · ib_async · asyncio · JSONL event sourcing · Windows Server · IB Gateway

The live bot doesn't re-implement the strategy. It imports the backtester's own decision code, pinned to an exact git SHA that it verifies at startup and stamps into every recording.

Every session is recorded as an append-only event log. The recording is replayed through the backtester and diffed decision by decision. A different gate verdict, trigger, stop or share count is a Class-0 divergence, and that's a bug by definition, not a tolerance. It ran first as a read-only observer, recording every decision and placing nothing; order code came after.

  • Budgeted attention. IBKR allows about 100 market-data lines and only three tick-by-tick streams. A tiered watchlist maps onto those limits exactly: every scanner mover monitored, confirmed movers on 5-second bars, and the three tick slots reserved for open positions and armed setups.
  • The bot is the stop. IBKR honours no stop orders premarket, so stops are synthetic: watched on every tick and fired as marketable limits. While a position is open, the process being up is the stop. So it adopts positions back after a crash.
  • Refuses rather than guesses. It won't arm on anything but a paper account, won't start on a SHA or strategy mismatch, and a kill file flattens everything on the next loop.

The 88-second phantom

The bot booked a winning exit that the broker hadn't filled yet.

  1. 06:40:39.079Trigger fires on WETO. Long entry submitted.
  2. 06:40:40.706Filled: 242 shares at 8.7731. Stop 8.34, target 9.45 resting.
  3. 06:41:44.143A print at 9.47 crosses the target. The bot books +$168.66 and drops the position from its book.
  4. 06:41:45.614reconcile_alarm: "broker holds 242 WETO with NO position in the book". Thirteen times.
  5. 06:43:12The resting target finally fills. For 88 seconds a live position sat outside the book: no synthetic stop, no crash adoption, and a resting order nobody would have cancelled.

The fix: decide on the touch, settle on the broker.

The exit decision still happens on the first touch, so parity with the backtest is unchanged. Only the bookkeeping now waits for the broker's fill. Fill quantities are read, never assumed, and every session ends with an audit of IBKR's own execution records against the bot's ledger.

def _arm_target_confirm(self, pos: Position, price: float) -> None:
    """The price touched the target. Record the decision -- but do NOT book
    the exit, because the resting limit is a real order at the exchange and
    only IT can say whether we are flat."""
    self._confirming[pos.key()] = (price, systime.monotonic())
    self.rec.event("target_touched", ticker=pos.ticker, touch=price,
                   target=pos.target_price, shares=pos.remaining)

Lumina

A makeup and retouching app that runs entirely on your phone. Your photo never leaves it.

  • Built withWebGL2 · GLSL · MediaPipe (WebAssembly) · vanilla JavaScript · no framework, no build step

Makeup, skin, sculpt, hair, relight, background and 85 film filters: 28 tools, built from scratch on WebGL2 and on-device models.

Lumina finds a face as 478 landmarks with depth, turns them into soft region masks through the canonical face mesh, and runs every effect as a GPU shader. Nothing is generated and nothing is uploaded. It's feature-matched against the leading commercial beauty apps, and only the tools that would need a server are left out.

This is the real app, not a video.

The phone on the left runs Lumina itself, loaded from this site. It opens on the sample with cached face data, so nothing heavy downloads until you open a photo of your own. On a phone, it's better full screen.

Open Lumina full screen ↗

  • Hair, strand by strand. A matting pass estimates each edge pixel's share of hair from where its colour sits between hair and background, so recolouring never leaves a halo.
  • Sculpt that never tears. Fifty face controls defined once on the canonical mesh, applied through 468 detected points, and rasterised into an exactly invertible warp grid with a fold guard.
  • Background, rebuilt on the phone. To move the subject without dragging the background, it rebuilds what was behind them: a push-pull fill, PatchMatch with Wexler-style voting, then full-resolution detail.
  • Light in 3D. Relight builds normals from an inflated silhouette with the face mesh's depth matched in, and lights in linear space so shading stays natural.
